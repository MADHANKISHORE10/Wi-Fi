1. Key features of Wi-Fi 6, 6E, and 7 & difference from Wi-Fi 5
•	Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac):
Operates only in 5 GHz, uses OFDM and supports downlink MU-MIMO. Focus is mainly on increasing peak data rate. 
•	Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax):
Operates in 2.4 & 5 GHz. Introduces OFDMA, uplink & downlink MU-MIMO, 1024-QAM, and Target Wake Time (TWT). Focus is on efficiency and handling dense networks. 
•	Wi-Fi 6E:
Extends Wi-Fi 6 into the 6 GHz band, providing wider channels, more spectrum, and less interference. 
•	Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be):
Introduces 320 MHz bandwidth, 4096-QAM, Multi-Link Operation (MLO), and enhanced scheduling. Focus is on ultra-high throughput and low latency applications. 

2. Role of OFDMA in Wi-Fi 6
OFDMA divides a wide channel into smaller Resource Units (RUs), allowing multiple users to transmit simultaneously within the same channel.
•	Reduces contention among users 
•	Improves spectral efficiency 
•	Lowers latency 
•	Supports high-density environments 
 Unlike OFDM (single user), OFDMA enables multi-user parallel transmission.

3. Benefits of Target Wake Time (TWT)
TWT allows devices to negotiate specific wake-up times with the access point.
•	Reduces idle listening → saves battery 
•	Minimizes collisions by scheduling transmissions 
•	Improves network efficiency 
•	Particularly useful for IoT devices and low-power sensors 

4. Significance of 6 GHz band (Wi-Fi 6E)
The 6 GHz band provides a new, clean spectrum for Wi-Fi.
•	Large contiguous bandwidth 
•	More non-overlapping channels (20/40/80/160 MHz) 
•	Minimal interference from legacy devices 
•	Supports high throughput and low latency 
 Ideal for high-bandwidth applications like AR/VR and 4K/8K streaming.

5. Wi-Fi 6 vs Wi-Fi 6E
Feature	Wi-Fi 6	Wi-Fi 6E
Frequency Bands	2.4 & 5 GHz	2.4, 5 & 6 GHz
Spectrum Availability	Limited	Expanded
Interference	Moderate	Very low
Channel Width	Up to 160 MHz	More clean 160 MHz channels
Performance	High	Higher due to less congestion

6. Major innovations in Wi-Fi 7 (802.11be)
•	320 MHz channels → doubles bandwidth 
•	4096-QAM → increases bits per symbol 
•	Multi-Link Operation (MLO) → simultaneous multi-band usage 
•	Enhanced MU-MIMO and OFDMA 
•	Lower latency and jitter 
Designed for real-time applications like gaming, AR/VR, and industrial control.

7. Multi-Link Operation (MLO)
MLO allows a device to use multiple frequency links (2.4, 5, 6 GHz) simultaneously.
•	Parallel data transmission 
•	Improved throughput 
•	Reduced latency 
•	Better reliability (failover capability) 
Overcomes limitations of single-band communication.

8. Purpose of 802.11k and 802.11v
•	802.11k (Radio Resource Management):
Provides information about neighbouring APs (signal strength, load), helping clients make better roaming decisions. 
•	802.11v (Network Assisted Roaming):
Allows APs to suggest better APs to clients for improved load balancing and performance. 

9. Fast BSS Transition (802.11r)
802.11r reduces roaming delay by enabling fast re-authentication.
•	Pre-establishes security keys 
•	Eliminates full authentication during handoff 
•	Reduces latency significantly 
 Critical for real-time applications (VoIP, video calls).

10. How 802.11k/v/r work together
These standards work together to provide efficient and seamless roaming:
•	802.11k: Supplies neighbour AP information 
•	802.11v: Guides client to optimal AP 
•	802.11r: Enables fast and secure transition 
Combined effect:
•	Faster roaming 
•	Reduced packet loss 
•	Improved user experience
